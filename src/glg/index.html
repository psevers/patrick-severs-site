02 · Wedge beyond trust and safety

Sell the work labs already buy in professional domains.

Public pattern: experienced practitioners author real work products, other practitioners judge them. That is the GLG wedge. Seen across GDPval-style and post-training evaluation work.

Finance + risk

Labs want: credit memos, investment memos, risk writeups, financial reasoning rankings, grounded research answers.

GLG builds: CFA/credit/IB-style cohorts grading model drafts and writing gold answers.

Legal + compliance

Labs want: brief quality, clause risk, policy exceptions, jurisdiction-aware critiques, escalation calls.

GLG builds: practice-area panels for preferred drafts, failure labels, and release gates.

Healthcare + LS

Labs want: care-plan quality, clinical reasoning, safety omissions, protocol adherence.

GLG builds: clinician/regulatory panels for gold outputs and critical-error flags.

Business ops

Labs want: realistic enterprise workflows, decision usefulness, tool-use judgment where humans score outcomes.

GLG builds: operator panels for tasks, edge cases, and trace review without owning the agent stack.

1

Tasks

Solutions builds: prompt, source pack, output format, edge cases. Must look like real work.

2

Gold answers

Experts + Delivery: decision-grade reference work and rationale a senior practitioner accepts.

3

Judgment

Delivery runs: blind rank/critique/correct, agreement check, adjudication on hard cases.

4

Holdout

Solutions + Delivery: unseen set for model comparison or release. Never mixed into training.

Buyers ask for sample data. That sample decides whether you get a pilot.

11 · Path to $100M

Go big with 1–2 labs. Do not build a balanced logo fantasy.

Concentration is the honest bet. Underwrite it and manage the risk in the open.

How we get there

  • 1–2 anchor labs become the majority of revenue
  • Expand by team / wedge / program inside those accounts
  • A few secondary lab programs for diversification
  • Enterprise as a smaller recurring lane after the factory works

What has to be true

  • At least one lab has expansion surface beyond T&S
  • Samples convert to pilots and quarterly POs
  • Accepted yield holds as volume rises
  • Working capital and concentration limits are explicit

Public ceiling signal: one Innodata account reached roughly $110.5M annualized in a year, with heavy concentration. Possible. Not automatic. Source: SEC filings.

No fake ramp. Day 90 evidence decides whether to double down on the concentration bet.
