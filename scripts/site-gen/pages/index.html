How I operate

Six rules. Each one has a receipt.

The AI-native part of the job is a set of operating rules I hold myself and my team to. The long version, including the agent fleet and the things built under my direction, is on the How I operate page.

  1. Direct, don't do.

    Agents write the code and the drafts. My job is to diagnose the actual problem, give clear direction, and hold the bar until the fix is proven.

    receipt: sent a "finished" internal tool back for a full rebuild because a clean screen was hiding broken states.

  2. Interview before you task.

    Context, role, interview, then task. Ask the AI to interview you before it builds anything. Credit to Geoff Woods, The AI-Driven Leader.

    receipt: this site was redesigned after a ten-question interview I had my AI run on me, one question at a time.

  3. Cost is policy, not preference.

    Model spend is governed in the build, not in a spreadsheet after the fact.

    receipt: the build fails if an unannotated top-tier model call shows up on a mechanical or scheduled path.

  4. A human approves every send.

    Agents draft. People decide what leaves the building. That rule has never had an exception.

    receipt: 0 autonomous sends across seven production agents.

  5. Presence beats outbound.

    Be where the buyers already are, with the expert and the sample in hand. Spend the agent savings on being in the room.

    receipt: events were 59% of G2i's Q2 2026 leads. Local-first beat email at The Agent Company.

  6. Catch what fails quietly.

    Hard failures announce themselves. The expensive ones don't. Guards, postmortems, and monitors that get checked against reality.

    receipt: a nightly pipeline had 404'd silently for weeks while monitoring showed green. Root-caused and fixed, monitor included.
