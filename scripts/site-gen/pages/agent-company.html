01

Day one.

A working product, a serious engineering team, and almost no go-to-market. That's the normal shape of a technical founding team, and it's the shape I'm most useful in.

Systems

No CRM. No lead source.

Outreach ran through several pieces of stitched-together tooling with no system of record behind them. No one could answer "who have we talked to and what happened."

Offer

No decision on what to sell.

The platform could build many things. Without a named problem and a named buyer, every conversation started from scratch and none of them compounded.

02

What got built.

The operating layer, sized for a startup: a system of record, a lead motion, an outbound program, and the demos and pages that force the product decision.

45-day build
the product decision came out of the sales motion, not ahead of it. Building the demo is how we found out what people would pay for.
03

What it produced, and what I'd tell you about it.

Five demos, four pilots. From a standing start, with no inbound and no brand. The demo-to-pilot rate is the number that matters at this stage. Pipeline volume isn't.
Local and in-person outperformed outbound email. Same offer, same pages. Being in the room moved the response rate more than any copy change did. G2i's events channel had already taught me that.
Priced to learn. Pilots are cheap so the constraint is projects built, not deals closed. The price gets revisited once the pilots show which problem is worth a full one.
What's not proven yet. Pilot-to-paid conversion, retention, and whether the outbound agent earns its keep once the local channel saturates. Those are the next 45 days.
Why it's on this site. It's the job I'm interviewing for, done in public, with a date on it: diagnose the business, decide what to sell, build the operating layer, get in the room.
