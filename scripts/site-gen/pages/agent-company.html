Day one

A working product, a serious engineering team, and almost no go-to-market.

That's the normal shape of a technical founding team, and it's the shape I'm most useful in.

No CRM. No lead source.

Outreach ran through several pieces of stitched-together tooling with no system of record behind them. No one could answer "who have we talked to and what happened."

No chosen product.

The platform could do many things; nobody had picked which one to sell first. Without a named problem and a named buyer, every conversation started from scratch and none of them compounded.

What got decided

The product decision came out of the sales motion, not ahead of it.

Building the demo is how we found out what people would pay for. Four moves in 45 days, in the order they happened.

  1. System of recorda CRM set up in week one. Agents did the wiring and the migration.
  2. Local-first lead motionin person, local events, warm intros. The channel that answered.
  3. Agent-run outbounddrafted by an agent, sent by a person, against prebuilt landing pages.
  4. Demos and solution pagesthree named problems, three pages, a demo path with a pipeline behind it.

Local beat email on response rate, by a lot.

What it produced

Five demos, four paid pilots, and what I'd tell you about them.

Five demos, four paid pilots.

From a standing start, with no inbound and no brand. The demo-to-pilot rate is the number that matters at this stage. Pipeline volume isn't.

Local and in-person beat outbound email.

Same offer, same pages. Being in the room moved the response rate more than any copy change did. G2i's events channel had already taught me that.

Pricing.

Paid pilots, priced so the constraint is projects built, not deals closed. The price gets revisited once the pilots show which problem is worth a full one.

What's not proven yet.

Pilot-to-paid conversion, retention, and whether the outbound agent earns its keep once the local channel saturates.

Why it's on this site.

It's the job I'm interviewing for, done in public, with a date on it: diagnose the business, decide what to sell, build the operating layer, get in the room.
